Codes that live
are read together.

We are the research group of Erik Garrison. Genomes are codes — four letters deep and billions long — and for most of the history of genomics we have read them one at a time, against a single privileged reference. We build new representations of living systems: whole populations of complete genomes, learning systems that grow out of sequence, and science itself as an inspectable computational object. When the inherited representation is blocking the view, we replace it — then follow the consequences.

[01] method

Representation is a scientific constraint.

Every result in genetics arrives filtered through a representation. When the filter destroys structure, the science goes blind in exactly the places the interesting questions live. So the lab's recurring move is the same at every scale of its work:

Do not throw away the structure too early.

Twenty years of that move makes a chain, and each link removes an inherited approximation:

2011 → freebayes — nearby alleles are independent · infer variation as haplotypes
2015 → vg — one genome is coordinate truth · represent alternative complete sequence paths, as a graph
2021 → pggb — one genome must be privileged · build pangenomes reference-free, from complete assemblies
2023 → impg — the graph must be materialized · the alignments themselves are the computational pangenome — make them queryable
2025 → impop — genomes become SNP matrices · population genetics over complete haplotype relationships
genome → population → research group → organization
The same question at every scale: what are the real entities, what are the relationships, what do our abstractions destroy — and what becomes possible when we keep more of the structure?

[02] work

Three directions, one thread.

02·a

Population genetics after the linear reference

Thousands of complete genomes are becoming ordinary, and the field's inference stack is still built to collapse them back into differences from one reference. We are building the other stack: IMPG makes all-versus-all alignments between assemblies queryable without materializing a giant graph; impop classifies sequence-sharing between genomes and paints ancestry and pedigrees across complete haplotypes; COSIGT genotypes complex loci at population scale from low-coverage sequencing — conceived here, developed with Davide Bolognini and Andrea Guarracino.

The same substrate keeps revealing biology the linear reference had hidden: exchange between acrocentric chromosomes, the formation and propagation of Robertsonian chromosomes, the concerted evolution of subtelomeres.

02·b

Learning over complete biological sequence

Representation is a constraint in machine learning too. Emender is a family of recurrent language models built from many small nonlinear matrix memories — a nonlinear delta-memory mechanism. Each memory head reads time sequentially, but hundreds of heads run in parallel across the GPU on a fused Triton kernel, which lets the recurrence train at billion-parameter scale: the opposite route from attention, taken seriously.

The longer aim is learning systems whose native object is complete biological sequence — populations of genomes as they are, not as flattened feature tables.

emender models on hugging face

02·c

Science as a computational object

Scientific work has an inherited representation as well — notebooks, meetings, chat threads, tacit knowledge in people's heads — and it destroys the structure that makes work verifiable. wg is an open-source coordination graph in which humans and AI agents work in one dependency graph: claims, handoffs, execution logs, artifacts, and histories preserved for review. Around it we co-founded Poietic PBC, a public-benefit company, with Luca Pinello (MGH/Harvard/Broad) and Vaughn Tan (UCL). This lab runs on it.

Call it the social science inside of our science.

[05] people

A small lab, hands on.

Erik Garrison

principal investigator · he/him · memphis & the network

Tenured associate professor of Genetics, Genomics & Informatics at the University of Tennessee Health Science Center. PhD in genomics at the University of Cambridge / Wellcome Sanger Institute — Graphical pangenomics, with Richard Durbin — after beginning FreeBayes in Gabor Marth's lab at Boston College and working on production and analysis for the 1000 Genomes Project. Before that: Harvard, social studies — the detour that later became the return. Co-chair of the Pangenomes Working Group, Human Pangenome Reference Consortium.

Around the PI: a deliberately small, distributed group of doctoral students, postdocs, and close collaborators across institutions. The lab is physical at UTHSC in Memphis and virtual everywhere else. It runs on wg — the work is meant to be inspectable by design.
